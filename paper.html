Paper I

Hunting for the Most Metal-Rich Stars in the Solar Neighborhood with High-Resolution Spectroscopy

Saad, Rowan, Stanek, Ilyin, Fulton, Isaacson & Lu

We present PANTERA (Project for Astrophysical Nucleosynthesis and Targeted Exploration of metal-Rich Abundances), a high-resolution spectroscopic survey of the most metal-rich stars in the solar neighborhood. In this first paper we report iron abundances for 56 metal-rich stars selected from Gaia DR3 XP spectrophotometric metallicities, observed with PEPSI on the Large Binocular Telescope, the Levy spectrograph on the Automated Planet Finder, and HIRES on Keck. We measure [Fe/H] from an equivalent-width analysis of iron lines, with the effective temperature from photometry and the surface gravity from the Gaia parallax, and validate the measurements against benchmark stars, cross-instrument pairs, a second synthesis code, and an independent equivalent-width measurement.

We find that the Gaia-XP metallicities over-predict [Fe/H] for part of the sample: consistent for the dwarfs, but reaching 0.16 ± 0.02 dex for the cool giants. We attribute the over-prediction in part to the strong blue line blanketing of the cool metal-rich giants and to the survey labels on which the XP metallicities were trained. We also find 25 of the 56 stars to be ultra-metal-rich ([Fe/H] > 0.4), with the most iron-rich reaching [Fe/H] = +0.58. We discuss the implications for studying metal-rich populations.

Keywords: stellar abundances · metallicity · Galactic chemical evolution · high-resolution spectroscopy

Data & code

Equivalent widths and atomic data for all 62 spectra, the adopted parameters and iron abundances, the six-dimensional kinematics with derived orbits, and the equivalent-width-cap sensitivity tables are public. Reduced spectra are available on request.
